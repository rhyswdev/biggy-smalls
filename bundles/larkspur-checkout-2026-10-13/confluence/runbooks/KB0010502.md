# KB0010502: PAY-2231 PayCore client resilience

Space: PAY · Owner: Payments Engineering · Last updated 24 Sep 2026 by Dana Whitfield

Design note for PAY-2231, shipping in payments-gateway 3.8.0. Reviewed by Priya Raman and Tom Beckett.

## Problem

In July (INC0045210) PayCore had a 40 minute outage and every authorization failed fast at our 3000 ms client timeout. That part worked as intended. The problem is the normal day: authorizations routed to PayCore's EU edge regularly take 2 to 3 s, and about 0.4 percent of them hit the 3000 ms timeout and are declined to the customer even though PayCore would have approved them a moment later. Support sees these as "card declined" complaints from customers whose card is fine.

## Change

- Raise `payments.psp.timeout-ms` from 3000 to 8000 so slow-but-successful EU calls complete.
- Retry a timed-out or 5xx authorize up to 3 times with backoff 1 s, 2 s, 4 s (`payments.psp.retries=3`, `payments.psp.backoff-ms=1000,2000,4000`).
- Send the order id as PayCore's idempotency key on every attempt, so a retry never authorizes twice.

The retry wraps the existing PayCore call inside `PaymentService.authorize`. Nothing else in the authorize flow changes: the same transaction still writes the `authorization_attempt` row, so audit and settlement are unaffected.

## Risk

- Worst case for one authorization during a PayCore outage is 4 x 8 s plus 7 s of backoff, 39 s. checkout-api's own client timeout to the gateway is 10 s, so checkout gives up first and shows the customer an error, which is what happens today.
- Load on PayCore during an outage rises by up to 4x on the failing share. PayCore's limit for our account is well above our peak.
- Settings are in `application-prod.yml` and can be reverted with a config-only release.

## Rollout

Staging soak planned from 12 Oct. Production in the 3.8.0 release under CHG0031877, rolling across pay-01 to pay-04.
