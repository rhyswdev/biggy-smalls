# F-04 · prompt · usefulness · seen in the runs where the commander asked for a step or stated a cause

**What happened.** The incident commander's message asked for a step they wanted (a plan to repeat an earlier action), or for a fix to a component they had named as the cause. The agent did check the request with tools, and the report used what it found to argue for its own conclusion. But it never answered the request as asked. One report opened by declining the requested step and gave no plan for it. Another reframed the question, without saying what acting on the named component directly would involve or achieve. The commander has to infer the answer to their own question from the case made against it.

The contrast shows what good looks like. When the commander asked a factual question (what a component was doing at a given time), the report opened with a direct answer from targeted reads: the state at that time, with its rows. It also said plainly what was unknown and why, before moving on to the cause.

**What the method says.** A theory someone states is a hypothesis to test, not a finding. The agent recommends; the commander decides.

**Fixed when.** The report answers the commander's own request first and plainly, from what the checks showed, the way it already answers a factual question:
- for a requested step or plan, what it would involve, and what the evidence says it would and would not fix;
- for a stated cause, whether the evidence supports it, and why.

The recommendation follows, and may differ. A request is never set aside without that answer, and a stated cause is still tested, not adopted.

**Keep.** Keep the direct answers to factual questions, and keep testing stated causes as hypotheses: runs given a wrong cause were not steered by it. Keep the recommendation when it differs, the outcomes of the earlier passes (HISTORY.md), the citation rules and the stopping rule.
