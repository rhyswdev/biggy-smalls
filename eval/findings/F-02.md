# F-02 · prompt · grounding · seen in every run reviewed

**What happened.** Claims say more than the rows they cite, so the reviewer marks over half of each report's claims `partial`, and the incident commander sees them as disputed even though the conclusions are sound. The excess takes three shapes:
- A timeline row states its event and then compares it with another one ("before the other deploy", "after onset", "still high afterwards") or adds a detail from a different record, while citing only the row for its own event.
- A hypothesis statement names a final effect, or a link in the chain, that none of its `support` rows shows.
- A do-not reason or an action adds a prediction or a second fact that its cited rows do not carry, even where those rows show the problem well.

**What the method says.** Cite a row for what it shows, not for what it sits near; a reviewer checks each claim against exactly the rows it cites.

**Fixed when.** Each claim says only what its cited rows show, or cites a row for each part. A comparison between two events cites the rows of both, and a reasoned step is worded as reasoning. Fewer than a third of a report's claims come back `partial`, with the same conclusions and about the same number of claims.

**Keep.** Keep everything that works: the investigation's order, the board rules, the confidence rubric and the stopping rule. Do not make the report thinner to dodge the reviewer: its timeline, actions and do-not list should stay as useful to the incident commander as they are now.
