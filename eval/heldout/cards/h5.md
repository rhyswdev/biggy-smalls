# h5: logged out at checkout (the cause is a job that stopped running)

**Status:** card only. Estimated 4 h plus the one-off new-date generalisation if h3 was not built first.

**Truth.** Tuesday 20 Oct 2026, checkpoint 14:20. Sessions (30-minute sliding TTL) and persistent carts (`cart:{customerId}`, no TTL, the only copy) share the sessions Redis cluster: 3 primaries x 4 GB, `maxmemory-policy volatile-lru`. The daily `commerce-batch/cart-sweeper` Jenkins job (07:00, deletes carts idle over 30 days) last ran on 12 Oct (#1416); it has silently not run since (why is an open question: the timer trigger was lost in a Jenkins plugin upgrade on 13 Oct). Keys without TTL grow about 0.33 GB per weekday; demand passed 12 GB on Sunday. Redis evicts only TTL keys, which are the sessions. Effective session life falls to about 24 minutes Sunday, 16 Monday, under 8 by Tuesday 14:00. Idle customers are logged out and guest carts vanish. Synthetics stay green because they never idle.

**Decoys.** checkout-api 2.15.0 that morning bumped spring-session (matches time, topology and mechanism; a May incident about sessions was fixed by rollback). The six-day-old redis fragmentation flap teaches "redis alerts are noise". Harmful fixes: flush, switch to `allkeys-lru`, restart redis.

**Lessons this makes false.** "Redis is a red herring", "the cause is a change", "a green synthetic means customers are fine".

**Expected output.** Cause: evictions of session keys because untouched cart keys filled memory after the sweeper stopped. Trigger: memory crossing maxmemory. Actions: run the sweeper catch-up now (about 15 minutes); raise maxmemory to 5 GB per primary as a stopgap; do not roll back 2.15.0, flush, restart redis or change the policy; restore the sweeper schedule and alert on its last success and on evictions; customer update. Discriminators: evictions and tickets predate the deploy; the log signature is "cookie present, session not found" rather than May's "no SESSION cookie".
