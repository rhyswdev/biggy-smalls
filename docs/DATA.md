# Data note

The data note for the write-up (PLAN section 11) is written at step 10. This file starts with the decisions taken while generating the data, so they can be defended later.

## Decisions made during generation

Each entry is the smallest decision that resolved a gap or a conflict in `docs/PLAN.md`, with the reason.

### Contracts (step 1)

1. **Slack handles use the full channel name** (`slack:inc-checkout-latency/8b2e11f0`, not `slack:inc/…`). The short form in the plan's example is ambiguous as soon as two channels share a prefix; the hash already includes the channel.
2. **Handle prefixes for records the plan's grammar does not name**: `snow:jn/`, `snow:audit/`, `snow:ci/`, `snow:rel/`, `snow:user/`, `snow:group/`, `dt:ent/`, `pd:log/`, `pd:svc/`, `pd:ep/`, `pd:oncall/`, `dd:syn/`, `spl:alert/`, `spl:search/`, `k8s:`, and `jk:<job>#<n>/<sha8>` for a commit. All are listed in `smalls/contracts/handles.py`.
3. **A multi-line log record is one manifest row and one handle.** The handle hashes the whole record (first line plus continuation lines). `locator` is the 1-based line of its first line.

### Schema authorities (step 1b)

4. **OpenAPI 3.0 to JSON Schema.** `nullable` becomes a type union; an undiscriminated `oneOf` is validated as `anyOf`, because PagerDuty's spec declares overlapping channel types that no real payload can satisfy strictly.
5. **Fields the specs do not allow to be null are omitted rather than rendered null**: Datadog event `host` and `device_name`, metric series `unit`. PagerDuty incidents always carry a priority object and on-call rows always carry a schedule reference, because the spec types those as objects.
6. **Jenkins `url` and `actions`.** `url` is exported by `Run.getAbsoluteUrl()` under the name `url`; `actions` comes from `Actionable`, outside `Run.java`. Both are allowed.

### Scenario (step 2)

7. **Sampling rates are tuned to hit the line targets.** The plan's illustrative rates (request INFO 1 in 50, DEBUG flood 1 in 200, nginx 1 in 50, and so on) produce several times the targets: at 14 authorizations per second the gateway alone would emit about 1,760 sampled request lines. The targets and ceilings are kept; the rates in `scenario/larkspur-checkout.yaml` `sampling:` are what the data note will state. Sampling is deterministic (every Nth occurrence per class and host).
8. **Housekeeper stats are sampled to the "After cleanup" line**, every 30 s per host from 14:25 to 15:10 and every 5 min otherwise. HikariCP logs a Before and an After line every 30 s; both at full rate would be about 1,700 lines on their own.
9. **Stack traces are capped at 12 frames** by declaring `%ex{12}` in the gateway's custom `logback.xml` pattern, a common production setting. Uncapped Spring traces would be over 100 lines each.
10. **Structured records counted against the 250 ceiling** are the categories in the plan's size-budget sentence: alerts (Datadog events, monitors and synthetics, Splunk alerts, SolarWinds alerts), Dynatrace problems, ServiceNow incidents, journal entries, audit rows and changes, Jenkins builds, CIs, relationships, ServiceNow users, and PagerDuty objects. Slack messages, commits, metric responses, status snapshots, saved searches, Dynatrace entities, Slack users and channels, ServiceNow groups and Confluence pages are counted and reported but not held to the ceiling. The plan's counting-rules sentence also lists messages and commits, but its own per-source targets already sum past 250 if they are included, while the size-budget list sums to about 210.
11. **The Datadog events file is the stream export from 2026-10-12 18:00 UTC to 15:30 on the day.** A two-hourly redis flap over six days would be about 140 events against a target of about 40. The six-day history stays visible through the hourly `redis.mem.fragmentation_ratio` series and the monitor's creation date; the export carries about ten flaps.
12. **PayCore EU latency at baseline is 1.5 to 2.8 s per attempt.** The plan gives fast calls as 400 ms and also uses 3 s per EU call in the hold-time model. Non-EU calls render at 280 to 560 ms; EU calls render slower so that, with application and database work, an EU hold is about 3 s, which is what the hold-time model and the Little's law lint use.
13. **A request is either slow on every attempt or on none.** Slow share is the share of authorizations on the retry path; every attempt of a slow request times out at 8 s (retries go to the same EU edge), so it holds its connection for the full 39 s, fails, and trips leak detection at 30 s.
14. **The disk-space fire-and-resolve on ord-01 is PagerDuty 8810 at 13:52.** The plan fixes 8811 to 8815; a sixth incident must take a number that keeps numbers in time order, so the defect happens before 14:10.
15. **Customer-raised incidents: 3 between 14:41 and 14:50 (INC0048812 to INC0048814) and 8 from 15:05 (INC0048815 to INC0048822)**, as in the timeline. The source table's "8 customer" counts only the late batch.
16. **`sys_user` holds the 10 people and 2 integration users; `sys_user_group` holds 7 teams.** The source table says 6 users and 3 groups, but the people table says every person is a ServiceNow user and names seven teams.
17. **Per-VM pool sizes** (not given by the plan) are chosen so the totals match section 1.3 exactly: pay-02 reaches 35 at 14:31; at 14:33 three VMs are at 35 and pay-04's growth to 32 is refused; pay-04 takes the Datadog agent's freed slot at 14:34.
18. **F5 flaps**: ten down/up pairs across all four members from 14:36:40 to 15:05:05, pay-03 most often, each member drained about 90 s (in-flight transactions finish, its connections go idle, health passes, it is re-enabled).
19. **SolarWinds** has two pay-03 component alerts (14:40:45 and 14:53:50 UTC, rendered in Eastern) and one unrelated win-rpt-07 volume alert.
20. **Cart ids are in the submit request's query string** (`POST /api/checkout/submit?cartId=…`), so repeated carts are visible in nginx as well as in checkout-api's `cartId` field.
21. **Monitor thresholds**: p90 on payments-gw alerts above 6 s (`max(last_5m)`); p99 warns above 8 s and would alert above 15 s, which the 10 s client timeout makes unreachable, so it stays at Warn; the success-rate monitor (full bundle) alerts below 95 percent from 14:34:30 to 15:28:30.
22. **The whole timeline to 15:30 is generated**, including the 15:15 stretch items (rollback #413, CHG0031910, PayCore v2 and v3, late customer incidents, the 14:56 kill), because the log windows run to 15:30 and logs that stop at 14:52 would be inconsistent with every other source. The replay clock hides them before their checkpoint.
23. **All three triggers are written** as raw inputs (`triggers/1440.md`, `1452.md`, `1515.md`).

### Gate A (agreed)

24. **Visibility is decided on `t_known`, not `t_utc`.** The manifest gains a required `t_known` column: the earliest time an observer could see the record. It equals `t_utc` except for backdated records (CHG0031910 opened 15:09 for a 15:01 deploy; PayCore v2's incident `started_at` 14:24 inside a snapshot published 15:10:22) and metric buckets, which are known when they close. Without it a timeline sorted by event time leaks backdated records into earlier checkpoints. The rules are `known_rules:` in the scenario YAML; the leak test and the scorer's "future" check use `t_known`. From the ingestion options draft, decision 3.
25. **Source clocks ship as a served `integration.yaml`** at the bundle root, rendered from `facts.yaml` `zones:` (zone, format, marker; notes dropped). ServiceNow display values and SolarWinds times carry no zone marker, so ingestion cannot read them without configuration, and reading `facts.yaml` would be reading the oracle. In a real estate this is integration config: Splunk props.conf `TZ`, the ServiceNow integration user's time zone, the Orion server's zone. It is not a vendor source and does not count against the budget. From the ingestion options draft, decision 4.

### Step 6 data fixes (`docs/decisions/step6-decisions.md` section 3)

41. **Fix 1: one constant rate per line class, kept per cart.** Entries 7, 8 and 29 are superseded. checkout-api decides per cart by hashing `cartId` and the decision travels with the call to the gateway (head-based sampling), so a kept cart keeps every line of every one of its requests in both logs, retries and resubmits included. Rates nest: a cart kept at 1 in 400 is kept at 1 in 100. The cart rather than the order is the key because resubmits get new order ids, and keying on the order would lose the "same cart 2 to 5 times" discriminator (57 carts now repeat in the served checkout errors).
42. **Fix 1 rates**, held for the whole window (served in `integration.yaml` `sampling:`): authorize ok 1 in 1,500; slow PSP response 1 in 12; retried requests (3 WARNs and the final ERROR) 1 in 100; pool timeouts 1 in 200 (1 in 100 put payments 300 lines over its budget; the first kept timeout per host still prints the full cause chain); leak traces 1 in 400 as ruled (19 traces, 3 to 5 per host across the incident); fail-fast 1 in 60; housekeeper stats one 30-second run in 4 (every 2 minutes per host, replacing the dense 14:28 to 14:45 window, which itself showed as a jump in a timechart); pool growth 1 in 6; pay-04's "Cannot acquire" every tenth-second attempt 1 in 24; health failures 1 in 14; checkout gateway failures 1 in 100 per cart; checkout request lines 1 in 2,000; the 2.14.3 DEBUG flood 1 in 70 of a true 1,680 lines a minute; Postgres refusals 1 session in 12 (was all for the first minute); EOF at restart 1 in 8.
43. **Anchors: at most one force-kept request or record per host**: ord-7781901 on pay-01 (retry, leak trace and its checkout 504), the first pool timeout on pay-02, the first "Cannot acquire" on pay-04 and the first 53300 on pay-db-01. The pay-02 pool timeout is pinned in the request stream, not just in the log: pay-02 cannot time out a request before 14:31:07.221, and the pinned request's checkout 502 carries the same order. The 14:31:34 stats line is on pay-02's 2-minute grid, so it is not an extra anchor.
44. **Every Postgres connection pair is logged**: gateway pool growth (113 between 14:29 and 14:34, against the reconciler's 40), the five `minimumIdle` connections each gateway opens on its 3.7.9 restart, the 30 refills after the 14:56 kill, every order-service reconnect on `maxLifetime`, and the Datadog agent reconnecting at 15:08 (budget row "other" 0 to 1). 45. **Log line targets are re-based** to payments 1,950, checkout 1,300, Postgres 650, nginx 570, F5 20, k8s 60: 4,550 against the 5,000 ceiling (about 9 percent under, not 15). nginx is not built yet; its target shrinks so the full bundle still fits.
46. **Invariant 15 tests shape at the width a correct sampler can pass** (agreed on 2026-10-13). A literal per-minute Spearman above 0.8 is out of reach at the ruled rates: a Monte Carlo of a correct constant-rate sampler gives a 5th-percentile rho of about 0.5 for retries and checkout timeouts, 0.2 for leak traces and 0.4 for refusals, because the curves plateau and sparse samples scramble ranks within the plateau (Pearson is no better). So the lint (`smalls_data/shape.py`) runs two tests against the unsampled population the generator writes to `_internal/population.json`: kept units per 10-minute bucket and in total sit inside a 4-sigma binomial band of population / N, which fails at once on a rate that changes mid-window (the defect fix 1 removed); and Spearman above 0.8 over 14:20 to 15:10 at the narrowest of 1, 2, 5 or 10-minute buckets at which a correct sampler passes in 95 percent of draws. Classes too sparse for any width (retries, leak traces, checkout timeouts, refusals) get the band only; the lint lists them.
47. **The served `sampling:` block names line classes in pipeline terms** (`psp_retry`, `pool_timeout`, `leak_detection`), with the regex a tool uses to classify a record's first line (first match wins), `keep_1_in`, the unit (`per: request | line | session | housekeeper run`) and the key. Invariant 15 checks every served log record matches the class that claims it, and that the served block equals the scenario's minus the generator class names. A Postgres `connection received` line belongs to the session of the line after it.
48. **A failed cart is resubmitted at most until its fifth submit** (PLAN 1.1, "two to five times"); the stream used to reuse a failed cart without limit (one cart failed 9 times). A fresh cart id can still collide with an earlier one in the 20,000-id range.

### Step 6a ingestion (`docs/decisions/ingestion-decisions.md`)

Judgment calls made while building the store and the adapters. The ruling record fixed the DDL and the ten store rules; these entries are what it left to the build.

98. **Store rows are records plus metric points, and the parity count is over both.** Each of the 1,407 metric buckets is a manifest row, so it is a `point` row: 4,454 `record` rows and 1,407 `point` rows make the 5,861. `point` gains a `handle` column (`metric:<query>`, the manifest's identity), because parity needs the handle on the row; `series` holds the series expression, so a query grouped by a tag keeps its series apart. A metric response's envelope has no manifest row, so it is a `ref` row named by its path, like the Slack users and channels files.
99. **Other additions to the ruled DDL**, none of them a change of meaning: `ref` gains `flags`, so a reference file can name its withheld fields like a record does; a trigger refuses a second `clock` row, so "zero or one row" is enforced by the database and not by convention; `visible_chunk` also returns the chunk's rowid as `chunk_id`, which is what joins it to `chunk_fts`; and seven indexes (handle, source and dataset, kind, parent, template, point series, chunk rid).
100. **The `kind` vocabulary**, extended from the ruled list: `log` (every `splunk/raw/*` record, Kubernetes events included), `alert` (a Datadog event, a Dynatrace problem version, a Splunk alert delivery, a SolarWinds alert), `alert_def` (a Datadog monitor or synthetic test, a Splunk saved search), `incident` (a ServiceNow incident and a PagerDuty incident; the source tells them apart, and a PagerDuty incident carries a `stream_key`), `change`, `build`, `commit`, `note` (a journal entry, a PagerDuty annotate entry: text a person wrote), `event` (the other PagerDuty log entries: the incident's machine timeline), `audit`, `message`, `doc`, `status`, `config` (PagerDuty services, escalation policies and on-call shifts), `ci`, `rel`, `user`, `group`, `entity`. `dataset` is the file-level set: the table for ServiceNow (the live incident's snapshots are `incident` too), the file stem for Datadog, Dynatrace and PagerDuty, the channel for Slack, `log` or `events` for raw logs.
101. **`sev` is a syslog severity number, lower is worse, and only where the source has a level of that kind**: log levels, the syslog word on an F5 line, a Kubernetes event's type, a Datadog event's `alert_type`, a SolarWinds severity, and an nginx line by its status class. Vendor priorities and categories (a ServiceNow priority, a PagerDuty urgency, a Dynatrace `severityLevel`) are not mapped onto it: a mapping between vendors' scales would be a judgment presented as a fact. They stay in `sev_text` as the vendor wrote them.
102. **Adapters are chosen by what the data is, not by this bundle's names.** A source with a `break_only_before` rule is a raw log, and its parser is picked by the shape of the file's first record (logback, logstash JSON, Postgres, nginx combined, BIG-IP syslog, Kubernetes Event), never by a sourcetype or folder name. A vendor export is named by its `sources:` key, which is the vendor's name. The status page is recognised by its `status.v<n>.json` files, because its key is the provider's own name and no adapter should carry that.
103. **A log record's `title` is the line its template is made from; `body` is the record as a person reads it.** For a text log both start with the first physical line. For a JSON log the title is rendered as `LEVEL logger - message` (a Kubernetes event as `Type Reason Kind/name: message`), since a masked JSON line would be one template per pod, and the body is the message followed by the `stack_trace` field, so both trace shapes fold the same way (store rule 8). The parsed fields go to `attrs`; `native` keeps the raw line for exact matching.
104. **The masker (ruling D6) is four rules**: an HTTP request with its status keeps method, path and status; any other double-quoted value is masked; any token that holds a digit is masked; masks joined only by punctuation or spaces collapse to one. The status is kept because it is the one number that says what kind of line it is: without it a 200 and a 504 on the same endpoint are one template. Result: 100 templates over 3,989 log records (payments 32, checkout-api 27, nginx 15, Postgres 13, Kubernetes 11, F5 2). `template_id` is `h8(sourcetype, mask(title))`, so a tool can show a template from one of its rows.
105. **`native` is the exact served span.** A record inside a JSON document keeps its bytes as served, indentation included, found by a small span scanner over the file (`ingest/base.py`), not by re-serialising. A whole-file record (a snapshot, a webhook body, a knowledge page) keeps the file's trailing newline; a line record does not carry the newline that ends it. The parity test checks all three shapes against the files.
106. **Three fields are withheld beyond the ruled list, by the same rule** (a served value that is not true at `now`). Slack `reactions`: they are added after a message is posted and carry no time, so they cannot be derived at `now` either (3 messages have them). SolarWinds `ResetDateTime`, `Acknowledged`, `AcknowledgedBy` and `AcknowledgedDateTime`: what happened to an alert after it triggered; the reset time is kept in `t_end`, which is what "active at `now`" is derived from, and the acknowledgement fields are empty in this bundle but would leak the same way. The metric envelope's `series[].pointlist`: points are read through `visible_point`, and the envelope would otherwise hand a tool every bucket to 15:30.
107. **A status-page snapshot is its own handle and its own event**, so, unlike the versions of one record, each keeps its own event time; it is valid until the next snapshot is published, and the first one has no `valid_from`, because it is the page as it already stood. This is the one first version with a null `valid_from` in the manifest (store rule 2).
108. **Links between records are resolved inside a source and stored in `parent`**: a journal entry and an audit row to their incident (through `sys_id`), a customer incident to its parent incident, a PagerDuty log entry to its incident, a commit to its build, a Slack reply to the message that starts its thread, a Datadog event to its monitor, a Splunk delivery to its saved search. Nothing is resolved across sources: `entity_raw` is the vendor's own string and `entity_id` stays empty until step 7. `stream_key` groups an alert stream: `monitor:<id>` (with the group for a multi-alert), a Dynatrace display id, a Splunk `sid`, a SolarWinds alert object, a PagerDuty incident key.
109. **The full-text table holds 81 chunks**: 41 from the eight knowledge pages, one per heading, the section heading prefixed with the page title; 12 close notes of incidents, with CI, category and close code; and 28 journal entries. Each chunk hangs off its own record version, so a note is searchable exactly when it is visible (the live incident's close note only once its last version is). The ruling named Confluence; we added the notes now because `search_knowledge` is specified over them and the chunk table is where ingestion puts prose.
110. **What the store half of the leak test scans, and its two exemptions.** At each core checkpoint it reads every column a tool may show of every visible row, `native` through `render_native`, and looks for machine-written times in every clock the bundle uses (ISO with an offset, wall-clock times read in the source's zone, epoch seconds and milliseconds, the nginx and F5 forms, a receipt stamp, the epoch inside a Splunk `sid`) and for the checkpoint's future tokens. It reads about 7,900 times at 14:40 and 10,900 at 14:52, in every source but the knowledge pages (which carry a date only), and none is at or after `now`. Exempt, as times known in advance: an on-call shift's `end` (the served-file lint's own exemption) and a change request's planned window, `start_date` and `end_date`; a change's actual times (`work_start`, `work_end`, `closed_at`, `sys_updated_on`) are not exempt and are scanned like any other. Two columns are the store's own and are never shown: `valid_to`, and a `t_end` at or after `now`.
111. **Visible rows**: 2,960 at 14:40 (2,167 records, 793 points, 72 chunks) and 4,006 at 14:52 (3,068 records, 938 points, 75 chunks), equal to the manifest under the same predicate, with one version at most of any record.
112. **Two limits of the visibility contract we found and did not change.** A versioned record is invisible for the instant `now` equals a save time, because the earlier version ends at `valid_to` and the later one is not yet known (`t_known < now`); no checkpoint sits on a save, and the leak test asserts it. And `ref` rows have no clock: the Slack channel list names the incident channel from the start, though it was created at 14:35. Both checkpoints are after that, and the tool that lists channels should list those with visible messages.
113. **Measured**: the store builds in about 215 ms (median of 7, tags off, this machine) for 5,861 rows from 141 files; two ingests give the same `iterdump()` hash; zero unparsed lines; every log record has a sampling rate. This replaces the 141 ms measured before the full bundle.

### Step 6a tagging (`docs/decisions/ingestion-decisions.md` section 4)

114. **What the tagger sees.** A request carries `{"kind", "text"}` built from one record's own text and one question, the facet's. There are 311 distinct units over the bundle: 100 log templates (the masked title; every record of a template shares it), 22 alert titles, 20 alert definitions (title and message or description), 23 incident tickets (short description and description), 81 chat messages a person wrote, 29 notes (28 journal entries and the one PagerDuty annotate entry), 15 change requests, 12 pipeline runs and 9 commits. Free text is clipped (600 characters after the title, 1,200 for a message or note) and the key is taken after clipping. An alert occurrence is tagged by its title alone, with a leading `[Triggered]` or `[Recovered on host:x]` removed, so every event of one alert shares a tag whatever its state; a PagerDuty incident is its alert's title and shares those units. The live incident's 15 versions are one unit, since its text never changes. A pipeline run's text is the job, its parameters and its cause, without the build number or the result, so both versions of a build and every run of a timer job share a unit. A bot post is `act=automated` by its subtype, in code; a system message (a channel join) is untagged.
115. **The hand-labelled sample is fixed before any answer exists.** 61 units, about 20 per facet: within each kind, the first units by unit id, a hash of the text (`smalls tag --sample`). We labelled each from its own text and the option meanings, with four rules written at the top of `eval/tags/labels.yaml`: only the text, never what we know about the incident (a deploy run is a release even when a later record shows it was the rollback); a text that does two things takes the label of what it leads with; the meanings are read as written (a commit that changes application code is not "a new version built or deployed", so it is `other` unless it is the release commit or merge); and `other` is a label like any other, for which the right outcome is no tag. The labels were committed (`e57dd22`) before the first `make tag` run (`2e48824`).
116. **The bundle-string list behind lint invariant 16** is built from `scenario/facts.yaml` by `smalls_data/prompt_lint.py`: the company and bundle names, each person's name, name parts, handle and display name, the integration users, the group names (whole, since their words are ordinary), every entity key and alias, hosts and addresses, and the named or versioned parameters; 162 strings, matched whole and in any case. The choice itself changed in 4 of 122 same-order repeats and 4 of 61 shuffled ones, and never where either side was above 0.62. So answers near 1 are steady and answers in the middle are not, which is what a floor is for.

It is an honest but narrow result: with 61 units, 0.93 passes and 0.94 does not (a tag at 0.95 in the cache came back at 0.93 in the shuffled repeat), so the number says "in the low nineties", not a sharp edge. Every facet reaches the bar, so all three stay offered as filters.
121. **Coverage over the whole bundle at 0.93**: 143 of 311 units tagged (46%), 154 answered below the floor and 14 answered `other`. By facet: `signal` 82 of 165 (log templates 51 of 100, alert titles 15 of 22, alert definitions 12 of 20, incident tickets 4 of 23); `act` 37 of 110 (chat messages 31 of 81, notes 6 of 29); `change_kind` 24 of 36 (change requests 11 of 15, pipeline runs 12 of 12, commits 1 of 9). In records: `signal` on 2,007 of 4,124 (routine 992, errors 563, saturation 162, latency 142, change 100, availability 46, traffic 2); `act` on 65 of 138, 28 of them `automated` by code; `change_kind` on 43 of 63 (release 33, job 6, network 2, rollback 1, infra 1). At 0.80 the unit figures would be 109, 61 and 24. With `SMALLS_TAGS=on` ingest has zero cache misses; the default stays off (ruling O2).
122. **Where the tags are thin, stated so nobody reads coverage as accuracy.** Texts that do two things split the probability and get no tag: a log line that is both a failed request and an exhausted pool answered `saturation` and `errors` at 0.50 each; "rollback done, still seeing 504s" answered `status` at 0.78. Customer tickets mostly answer `other` or low `errors`. Commits answer `other`, as we labelled them: the `change_kind` options describe change records and deploys better than code changes. None of this is a wrong tag, which is the error the floor exists to avoid; it is the cost of that choice, and rule T4's footer (how many rows matched without the tag filter, and how many are untagged) is what keeps it from hiding evidence.
